4Cast Privacy Policy

Effective October 23, 2026

4Cast is designed so that your data stays on your device. There are no accounts, no advertising, no analytics SDKs, and no tracking.

Location

With your permission, 4Cast uses your device location to fetch weather for where you are. Your coordinates are sent to Apple Weather (WeatherKit) to obtain forecasts.

From version 1.5, air quality comes from public services. Your coordinates are rounded to about one kilometer before being sent to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's AirNow service, which is hosted on Esri's ArcGIS Online, and to the Finnish Meteorological Institute. They are rounded to about ten kilometers before being sent to the Finnish Meteorological Institute's SILAM model server.

From version 1.5, the map's rain and its other layers come from the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, the Japan Meteorological Agency, EUMETSAT, and the Finnish Meteorological Institute. Those services receive the area of the map being shown, as map tiles and boxes tens of kilometers across or larger, not your coordinates. When the map shows the United States or its surroundings, the middle of the map, rounded to about one kilometer, is sent to the U.S. National Weather Service to look for warning areas.

From version 1.8, the map's radar over Canada north of 55°N and east of 60°W comes from Environment and Climate Change Canada, which receives the area of the map being shown, as map pictures, not your coordinates.

In versions before 1.5, radar, map layers, and air quality came from RainViewer and Open-Meteo, with coordinates rounded to about one kilometer, and the middle of the map, rounded the same way, was sent to the U.S. National Weather Service wherever the map was.

None of these requests include your name, account, or device identifiers beyond what a normal network connection provides.

Calendar

From version 1.7, if you turn on Your Day, 4Cast asks for access to your calendars and reads the events of today and tomorrow on your device. It never adds, changes, or deletes an event. To find the place of an event, 4Cast asks Apple Maps, as its place search does: the place as written in the event is sent, with the area around where you are so that the nearest match is found. The forecast for that place is then fetched from Apple Weather with its coordinates. Places that are video calls or web addresses are not looked up. Titles, times, notes, and the people invited stay on your devices, and nothing about your calendar is sent to the developer, who runs no server. From version 1.10, the Your Day widget and 4Cast on your Apple Watch show your next plans: for each plan with a place today or tomorrow, its title, time and place in a few words, with the weather there, are kept in a file on your iPhone that only 4Cast and its widgets can read, and sent to 4Cast on your Apple Watch, if you have one, over the iPhone's own connection to it. They are written again at each refresh, and emptied when you turn Your Day off. Notes and the people invited are never read into them. The places found and their forecasts are otherwise kept only while the app runs, and your limits for activities are stored with your settings on your device. You can turn Your Day off in 4Cast's Settings, and remove its access in the Settings app under Privacy & Security, Calendars.

Storm Watch

From version 1.8, tropical storms are read from the public advisories of NOAA's National Hurricane Center (with their cones from NOAA's map service) and of the U.S. Joint Typhoon Warning Center, and lightning from the files of NOAA's GOES satellites on Amazon Web Services, through NOAA's Open Data Dissemination. These are the same files for everyone, and the requests carry no location; the only choice the app makes is which of the two satellites to ask, the one that sees the place better. Whether a storm's winds or lightning come near your places is worked out on your device.

From version 1.9, NOAA's outlooks of severe storms and flooding rain for the days ahead (from its Storm Prediction Center and Weather Prediction Center) and of tropical storms that may form (from its National Hurricane Center) are read from their public files and NOAA's map service, the same for everyone, with no location; whether one of your places lies in an outlook's area is worked out on your device. For a storm near one of your places, 4Cast also reads the National Hurricane Center's files of the chances and arrival times of its winds. These are named by the storm and its advisory, not by a place, and are the same for everyone; asking for them shows only which storm is near one of your places, as a visit to the Center's page for that storm would.

Tides

From version 1.9, for a place in the United States or its territories within 20 kilometers of one of NOAA's tide stations, 4Cast asks NOAA's tide service (the Center for Operational Oceanographic Products and Services) for that station's predicted high and low tides. The list of stations comes with the app and the nearest one is found on your device: the request names the station, not your place's coordinates.

Saved locations and settings

Cities you add and your unit preferences are stored on your device. If iCloud is enabled, they sync between your devices through iCloud Key-Value Storage, which is covered by Apple's privacy terms. From version 1.10, when you remove a city, a note of it (where it was, and when it was removed) is kept in the same storage for six months, so your other devices remove it too rather than add it back.

Notifications

Severe weather alerts, precipitation notices, the daily briefing, sky moments, Your Day's note the evening before, the note the evening before a stormy day, and notes of lightning nearby are scheduled on your device from the forecast data, and Storm Watch's Live Activity is kept on your device. 4Cast does not operate a push notification server.

Summaries

Short forecast summaries are generated on your device with Apple Intelligence when available. If you enable the optional Private Cloud Compute fallback, the forecast facts (never your location history or identity) may be processed by Apple's Private Cloud Compute under Apple's privacy protections.

Data we do not collect

4Cast does not collect contact information, identifiers, usage data, diagnostics, or any information for advertising or tracking.
